The following sections set out a standardized method for generating an Alt-Hammer battle. You can use all of these methods, or a selection within, as best suits how you want to play your game.


## Determine Board Size Deployment Zones


Alt-Hammer battles are played on a tabletop game board that should align with the following dimensions as best as possible: 
- 500 Points or Less: 24" by 36"
- 501 to 1,000 Points: 36" by 48"
- 1,001 to 2000 Points: 48" by 72" 
- 2,001 to 3,000 Points: 48" by 96"
- Above 3,000 Points: Custom to suit your massive game!

## Determine Attacker, Defender, and Deployment Zones


With a game size and board size established, players must determine who will be the Attacker and Defender, and then determine which Deployment Zone will be used for the battle. 

To determine Attacker and Defender, each player rolls a D6. The player with the highest roll is the Attacker.

The Attacker then rolls a D6 to select which Deployment Zone will be used for the battle, the result of which corresponds to 1 of the following Deployment Zones:

It should be noted that these deployment zones are designed with a standard 2,000 points or less game and 2:3 game board ratio in mind, wherein a gap equal to one third of the total board length - being 24" on that 2,000-point game board size - equals the minimum distance between deployment zones along any frontage. Players should come to a mutually agreeable method of applying these deployment zones to larger Alt-Hammer games if using them is the plan. 


## Deploying Units


Players alternate deploying their units one unit at a time, starting with the Defender, until both players have deployed all units not placed into Strategic Reserves. Players can choose to place up to half of their units (rounding down) into Strategic Reserves.

Units set up in this way must start the battle in Strategic Reserves.


## Pregame Setup


Before the battle begins, players should complete the following additional setup steps.

Players should determine how terrain features will affect the game rules to ensure they are applied consistently. Players should discuss what types of terrain features are on the battlefield - Cover, Difficult Ground, Impassable Ground, etc. - and clarify how models move through, over, or around them.


## Primary Mission Objectives


Primary Mission Objectives are placed on the battlefield before Deployment. Primary Objectives represent key tactical locations that both armies are fighting to control.

### Determine Number of Primary Mission Objective Markers


The number of Primary Mission Objective Markers on the battlefield is determined by the game size, and should be set up before the Deployment step.

- 500 Points or Less: 3 Primary Objective Markers
- 501 to 1,000 Points: 5 Primary Objective Markers
- 1,001 to 2000 Points: 7 Primary Objective Markers
- 2,001 to 3,000 Points: 9 Primary Objective Markers

### Place Primary Mission Objective Markers on the Battlefield


Primary Objective Markers should be placed in mutually agreed-upon locations that are tactically interesting and not immediately accessible to either Deployment Zone. If players cannot agree on placement, the Attacker places Primary Objective Markers one at a time, alternating with the Defender.

Primary Objective Markers can be placed anywhere on the battlefield except within 6" of another Primary Objective Marker and within 9" of a battlefield edge.

### Holding Objectives


A player controls a Primary Mission Objective Marker if they have more models within range of it than their opponent. The range of a Primary Objective Marker is 3".

#### Models Embarked in a Transport


Models embarked in a Transport do not count for the purposes of controlling Primary Objective Markers.

### Victory Points for Controlling Objectives


At the end of each Battle Round, players tally their Victory Points. Players score Victory Points for each Primary Mission Objective Marker they control.

#### Deployment Zone Objective Markers


Objective Markers within a player's own Deployment Zone are worth 1 Victory Point each. Objective Markers in No Man's Land are worth 2 Victory Points each. Objective Markers within the enemy's Deployment Zone are worth 3 Victory Points each.


## Optional Game Feature: Determine Mission Rule


Players can utilize Mission Rules as an optional way to enhance gameplay for Alt-Hammer battles. To do so, each player rolls a D6 at the start of the game. Both players use the higher result to determine the Mission Rule in effect for the entire battle.


## Optional Game Feature: Secondary Mission Objectives


Players can utilize Secondary Mission Objectives as an optional way to enhance gameplay for Alt-Hammer battles. To do so, at the start of each Battle Round, each player rolls a D6 for each of the 3 Secondary Mission Themes and selects 1 result per Theme.

Secondary Mission Objectives that are not completed by the end of the Battle Round can be retained for the next Battle Round, but completed Secondary Mission Objectives must be replaced at the start of the subsequent Battle Round. Each time a player generates a Secondary Mission Objective that is impossible to complete by the end of the Battle Round — a fact which should be agreed-to by all players — the player can discard it and regenerate another to replace it.

Secondary Missions are organized into 3 Secondary Mission Themes: **Purge the Foe**, **Denial**, and **Reconnaissance**. Players roll a D6 for each Theme; the result determines which of that Theme's 3 objectives is in effect (1–2, 3–4, or 5–6).
