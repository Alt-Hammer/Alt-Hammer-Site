## Setting Up the Battlefield


The following sections set out a standardized method for generating a Countermarch battle. You can use all of these methods, or a selection within, as best suits how you want to play your game.


##### Determine Battle Size and Board Size


Countermarch battles are played on a tabletop game board that should align with the following dimensions as best as possible, which are determined by the army size (in terms of total points) that players have chose for the battle: 
- 500 Points or Less: 24" by 36"
- 501 to 1,000 Points: 32" by 48"
- 1,001 to 2000 Points: 48" by 72" 
- 2,001 to 3,000 Points: 48" by 96"
- Above 3,000 Points: Custom to suit your massive game!

##### Place Terrain Features


After determining battle size and battlefield dimensions to be used, it is recommended that players place terrain features on the board before proceeding with any other setup steps. This offers the opportunity for the relationship between terrain placement, <span class="attacker">Attacker</span>/<span class="defender">Defender</span> roles and deployment zone layouts to be completely random. Players should select from Countermarch's existing Terrain Types described under Terrain and/or or create custom terrain features using a combination of Terrain Attribute Keywords. In any case, it is recommended that all players have a complete understanding of each terrain feature's traits - both in terms of benefits and drawbacks - before proceeding with subsequent setup.


##### Determine Attacker, Defender, and Deployment Zones


With a game size and board size established, players must determine who will be the <span class="attacker">Attacker</span> and <span class="defender">Defender</span>, and then determine which Deployment Zone will be used for the battle. 

To determine <span class="attacker">Attacker</span> and <span class="defender">Defender</span>, each player rolls a D6. The player with the highest roll is the <span class="attacker">Attacker</span>.

The <span class="attacker">Attacker</span> then rolls a D6 to select which Deployment Zone will be used for the battle, the result of which corresponds to 1 of the following Deployment Zone Typologies: Sweeping Engagement, Force Recon, and Asymmetric Battle. With the Deployment Zone Typology selected, the <span class="attacker">Attacker</span> then rolls another D6, the result of which selects 1 of the 3 Deployment Zone layouts to be used for the battle. <h6 id="design-note">Design Note</h6>


It should be noted that these deployment zone layouts are designed with a standard 2,000 points or less game and 2:3 game board ratio in mind, wherein a gap equal to one third of the total board length - ie, 24" on that 2,000-point game board size - equals the minimum distance between deployment zones along any frontage. Players should come to a mutually agreeable method of applying these deployment zones to larger Countermarch games if using them is the plan. 


##### Deploying Units


Once Deployment Zones are identified, players alternate deploying their units onto the battlefield beginning with the <span class="attacker">Attacker</span>. Player's can alternate 1 unit at a time, or multiple units at a time at their discretion. 

Players must identify which of their units are either embarked in units with the <span class="keyword" data-term="transport-x" data-type="keyword">Transport [X] </span>ability, or placed in Strategic Reserves before placing any units on the battlefield.

Units deploying on the battlefield using the <span class="keyword" data-term="infiltrators" data-type="keyword">Infiltrators</span> ability must be deployed after all units that deploy normally have been placed. 


## Primary Mission Objectives


Every Countermarch battle played involves securing territory held by your enemy and defending territory you control, which can take the form of Primary Mission Objectives placed on the battlefield. This section explains how to determine your battle's Primary Mission Objectives.


##### Determine Number of Primary Mission Objective Markers


Players each roll a D6. The player with the highest roll then rolls a subsequent D6, the result of which is the total number of Primary Objective Markers that will be used for the battle. For games that are 1,000 points or less, it is recommended to consider the roll a D3 instead, to create a maximum of 3 primary objectives for these smaller battles (which use smaller battlefields).


##### Place Primary Objective Markers on the Battlefield


Beginning with the <span class="attacker">Attacker</span>, players take turns placing the Primary Objective Markers on the battlefield. Objective Markers are placed on the battlefield as follows: 
- Each player can only place 1 Objective Marker in their own Deployment Zone. Only the <span class="defender">Defender</span> can place the first Objective Marker in a Deployment Zone (either their own, of that of another player).
- An Objective Marker must not be placed within 6" horizontally of another Objective Marker.
- Objective Markers must be placed at least 9" away horizontally from any battlefield edge.
- Objective Markers in No Man's Land cannot be placed within 9" horizontally of a Deployment Zone's boundaries.

##### Holding Objectives


Securing and controlling objectives is the heart of victory in Countermarch 40,000. Every model in your army has an Objective Control (OC) value as determined by the following factors: 
- <span class="keyword" data-term="infantry" data-type="keyword">Infantry</span> and <span class="keyword" data-term="beast" data-type="keyword">Beast</span> models' OC value is equal to their Toughness characteristic
- <span class="keyword" data-term="monster" data-type="keyword">Monster</span> and <span class="keyword" data-term="vehicle" data-type="keyword">Vehicle</span> models with a Toughness characteristic up to 11 have an OC value equal to their remaining Wounds characteristic
- <span class="keyword" data-term="monster" data-type="keyword">Monster</span> and <span class="keyword" data-term="vehicle" data-type="keyword">Vehicle</span> models with a Toughness characteristic of 11 or higher have an OC value equal to double their remaining Wounds characteristic
- Models with the <span class="keyword" data-term="battleline" data-type="keyword">Battleline</span> keyword increase their OC value by 1
- Models with the <span class="keyword" data-term="character" data-type="keyword">Character</span> keyword increase their OC value by 3
- Models with the <span class="keyword" data-term="epic-hero" data-type="keyword">Epic Hero</span> keyword multiply their OC value by 2
- Models with the <span class="keyword" data-term="aircraft" data-type="keyword">Aircraft</span> keyword always possess an OC value of 0, and can never increase their OC value above 0
- Models that are Battle Shocked reduce their OC value to 1. No additional OC factors detailed above (such as possessing the Battleline keyword, for example) are added whilst that model is Battle Shocked.

To determine who controls a Primary Objective Marker, each player tallies up to the total Objective Control value for all of their models wholly or partially within 3" horizontally (and any distance vertically) of that Primary Objective Marker. The player with the highest Objective Control value controls that objective marker.


<h6 id="models-embarked-in-a-transport">Models Embarked in a Transport</h6>


Models that are embarked in a <span class="keyword" data-term="transport-x" data-type="keyword">Transport [X]</span> model do not use their normal Objective Control value as described above. Instead, each embarked model increases the Objective Control value of the <span class="keyword" data-term="transport-x" data-type="keyword">Transport [X]</span> model by 1.


##### Victory Points for Controlling Objectives


Successfully controlling Primary Objective Markers earns players a number of Victory Points as defined below. These Victory Points are tallied during Phase 3 of the Battle Round.

The Victory Point values earned are based on the number of Primary Objective Markers on the battlefield per below, as well as additional conditions detailed afterwards.


<h7 id="1-primary-objective-marker">1 Primary Objective Marker<span class="heading-value">24 Victory Points per Primary Objective Marker</span></h7>



<h7 id="2-primary-objective-markers">2 Primary Objective Markers<span class="heading-value">12 Victory Points per Primary Objective Marker</span></h7>



<h7 id="3-primary-objective-markers">3 Primary Objective Markers<span class="heading-value">8 Victory Points per Primary Objective Marker</span></h7>



<h7 id="4-primary-objective-markers">4 Primary Objective Markers<span class="heading-value">6 Victory Points per Primary Objective Marker</span></h7>



<h7 id="5-primary-objective-markers">5 Primary Objective Markers<span class="heading-value">5 Victory Points per Primary Objective Marker</span></h7>



<h7 id="6-primary-objective-markers">6 Primary Objective Markers<span class="heading-value">4 Victory Points per Primary Objective Marker</span></h7>



<h6 id="victory-point-modifications-for-deployment-zone-primary-objective-markers">Victory Point Modifications for Deployment Zone Primary Objective Markers</h6>


The <span class="attacker">Attacker</span>:
- Increases the Victory Points value of a Primary Objective Marker positioned within the <span class="defender">Defender's</span> Deployment Zone by 5.
The <span class="defender">Defender</span>: 
- Increases the Victory Points value of a Primary Objective Marker positioned within its Deployment Zone by 2.
- Increases the Victory Points value of a Primary Objective Marker positioned within the <span class="attacker">Attacker's</span> Deployment Zone by 2.

##### Optional Game Feature: Terrain Features as Primary Objective Markers


Players can choose to make 1 or more Terrain Features function as Primary Objective Markers. To do so, the following criteria must be satisfied:
1. The Terrain Feature must have an Area Footprint on the base of the battlefield designated (and, ideally, marked so that it is clearly visible) that is at least 3" long by 3" wide
2. The Terrain Feature must possess at least 1 of the following keywords: <span class="keyword" data-term="building" data-type="keyword">Building</span>, <span class="keyword" data-term="hill" data-type="keyword">Hill</span>, <span class="keyword" data-term="wreckage" data-type="keyword">Wreckage</span>
To determine who controls a Terrain Feature Primary Objective Marker, each player tallies up to the total Objective Control value for all of their models wholly within 1" horizontally (and any distance vertically) of the Area Footprint of that Terrain Feature Primary Objective Marker. The player with the highest Objective Control value controls that objective marker. 


<h6 id="randomly-generating-terrain-feature-primary-objective-markers">Randomly Generating Terrain Feature Primary Objective Markers</h6>


A suggested approach to utilizing Terrain Feature Primary Objective Markers is to do it randomly. After generating the total number of Primary Objective Markers the battle will include, players can then roll a D6 for each Primary Objective Marker. On a 4+, that Primary Objective Marker becomes a Terrain Feature Primary Objective Marker. Beginning with the Defender, each player then takes turn designating eligible Terrain Features that will be part of the battlefield.


<h6 id="placing-terrain-feature-primary-objective-markers-on-the-battlefield">Placing Terrain Feature Primary Objective Markers on the Battlefield</h6>


Another suggested approach for utilizing Terrain Feature Primary Objective Markers is to incorporate their placement onto the battlefield along with other Primary Objective Markers. To do so, simply follow the rules outlined in the 'Place Primary Objective Markers on the Battlefield' section, but with the following modifications:
- A Terrain Feature Primary Objective Marker must not be placed within 3" horizontally of another Objective Marker.
- Terrain Feature Primary Objective Markers must be placed at least 6" away horizontally from any battlefield edge.
- Objective Markers in No Man's Land cannot be placed within 3" horizontally of a Deployment Zone's boundaries.

## Optional Game Feature: Determine Mission Rule


Players can utilize Mission Rules as an optional way to enhance gameplay for Countermarch battles. To do so, one player rolls a D6. The resulting roll indicates which of the following Mission Rules will be in effect for the duration of the battle.


<h6 id="night-fight">Night Fight<span class="heading-value">D6 Roll: 1</span></h6>


Models can only make Ranged attacks against enemy units that are wholly within 18" of all models in the attacking unit during the first Battle Round.


<h6 id="surprise-attack">Surprise Attack<span class="heading-value">D6 Roll: 2</span></h6>


The <span class="attacker">Attacker</span> adds 2 to their Initiative Determination roll for the first Battle Round, and adds 1 to each successive Battle Round's Initiative Determination roll.


<h6 id="prepared-positions">Prepared Positions<span class="heading-value">D6 Roll: 3</span></h6>


The <span class="defender">Defender</span> adds 2 to their Initiative Determination roll for the first Battle Round, and adds 1 to each successive Battle Round's Initiative Determination roll.


<h6 id="phalanx-core">Phalanx Core<span class="heading-value">D6 Roll: 4</span></h6>


<span class="keyword" data-term="battleline" data-type="keyword">Battleline</span> units increase their Objective Control (OC) value by 2 instead of 1.


<h6 id="data-uplink">Data Uplink<span class="heading-value">D6 Roll: 5</span></h6>


Friendly units can attempt to complete the Data Uplink action. To do so, a unit must complete a <span class="keyword" data-term="mission-task" data-type="action">Mission Task</span> action while wholly positioned on a Primary Objective Marker controlled by the player. This action is successfully completed at the end of the Battle Round if the unit has not been destroyed, and the player still controls that Primary Objective Marker. If successful, gain 1 Command Point and 2 Victory Points.


<h6 id="battlefield-supremacy">Battlefield Supremacy<span class="heading-value">D6 Roll: 6</span></h6>


Each player selects 1 of the other Mission Rules to be in effect for the battle. 


## Final Step before Game Start


Once the Battle is generated, the following step is completed, which commences the battle:

**Determine Player Initiative for Battle Round 1:** Each player rolls a D6. The player with the highest roll takes the Initiative for the first Battle Round. Having the Initiative determines the order that the following items are completed, from highest Initiative to lowest:
- Pre-battle Rules that apply to a player's army (ie <span class="keyword" data-term="scout-x" data-type="keyword">Scout [X]"</span> moves)
- The Command Phase
- The first unit Activation in Battle Round 1 

Remember that Initiative is determined separately for each Battle Round, as detailed in Phase 3 of The Battle Round! 


## Optional Game Feature: Secondary Mission Objectives


Players can utilize Secondary Mission Objectives as an optional way to enhance gameplay for Countermarch battles and add interesting challenges that can evolve during the course of a battle. To do so, at the start of each Battle Round, each player can choose either to select 2 Secondary Mission Objectives at their discretion, or randomly select 3 Secondary Mission Objectives. These Secondary Mission Objectives will be in effect for the duration of the Battle Round. 

Secondary Mission Objectives that are not completed by the end of the Battle Round can be retained for the next Battle Round, but completed Secondary Mission Objectives must be replaced at that start of the subsequent Battle Round. For randomly-selected Secondary Mission Objectives that are determined to be impossible to complete by the end of the Battle Round - a fact which should be agreed-to by all players - the player can discard the Secondary Mission Objective and randomly determine another to replace it. 

Secondary Missions are organized into Secondary Mission Themes. Players can select 2 - or randomly select 3 by rolling a D6 - from any Theme.
